# Procedural creature ecosystem v1

The persistent animal is a versioned species recipe, individual variation and world state. No generated vertices, bones, textures, colliders or animation state enter world saves. This is an original implementation built for Forest Drift; no external creature generator, schema or assets are used.

## Architecture and shared contracts

`CreatureTypes.ts` establishes renderer-independent species/individual genomes, four body plans, parent-local semantic joints, swept body volumes, feature attachments, numeric compiled geometry, movement intent, spawn identity and persistent overrides. Coordinates are +Y up, +Z forward, +X right, with a logical ground root. `generatorVersion: 1` selects the current grammar; unsupported versions are rejected.

Implementation workstreams covered genomes/persistence, skeletons/geometry/skinning, procedural animation, ecology/behaviour/spatial indexing and settings. The integrating agent established contracts, reviewed the quadruped proof before ecology, built the Lab/runtime/world integration, and conducted browser and performance verification. The quadruped milestone compiled 100 seeds; animation validation covered 25 different quadrupeds before the other grammars were added.

`SpeciesGenerator` chooses a dominant silhouette strategy (long-legged, round, long-bodied or large-headed), a palette and correlated anatomy. Dimensions are reference metres, including approximately 0.12–25 m stature. `IndividualGenerator` makes bounded changes to size, proportions, palette, feature asymmetry and behaviour. Species identity remains recognizable. The existing named seeded RNG supplies every persistent procedural decision; the draw order of the species stream is part of identity, so later additions (the art-direction form and the colour-blocking palette) use their own named streams rather than inserting draws. Individual `sizeFactor` multiplies reference dimensions; `scaleRange` bounds that multiplier.

Each species also carries an art-direction **form** (`SpeciesForm`, `CreatureForm.ts`): a style preset (elegant, chunky, cute, slender, heavy), a torso profile (slender, deep-chested, round, pear, rear-/front-heavy, long, compact), one dominant, one secondary and sometimes a minor signature trait (huge ears, long neck, round belly, heavy haunches, dorsal fin, tall plates, snout, big eyes, curled tail…), and bounded ratios for attachment, limb, head, feature and colour-blocking choices. Traits add the anatomy features they need. The form is optional in saved recipes: recipes saved before it existed derive it with `deriveSpeciesForm`, the same function new species are generated with, so they compile identically.

## Compiler and animation

The quadruped has pelvis/chest limb pairs, spine, neck, head and tail. The upright biped has its own pelvis-to-chest axis, alternating legs and independent arms. Hexapods have three pairs around abdomen/thorax. Serpents use an 8–24 joint axial chain. Parent-local logical joints are converted into real `THREE.Bone`, `THREE.Skeleton` and one merged `THREE.SkinnedMesh` per creature.

Volumes are swept cross-sections along centripetal Catmull-Rom paths with rotation-minimising frames, monotone-cubic profiles (smooth bulges, no overshoot on tapers) and optional hemi-ellipsoid domes at either end. A path point may carry an offset from its bone, which is how roots start embedded inside their host. Torsos sweep along a straight spine line — on thick bodies a curved path folds rings over each other — and shape the back line, chest drop and belly with section offsets and separate upper/lower radii. Vertex normals, UVs, colours, per-vertex surface parameters and up-to-four-slot normalized skin influences are generated numerically.

LOD0, LOD1 and LOD2 tessellate the same anatomy: joints, attachments and ground plane are identical at every level, only radial sides (face 16/9/6, torso 12/8/6, limbs 8/6/4, small features 6/4/4), ring density and sub-silhouette detail (eye highlights, nose, toes, joint masses, every other element of unpaired feature rows) drop. Materials are shared, numeric geometry is kept separate from renderer allocation, and disposal releases geometry, skeleton textures and the material reference. The Lab uses its own material, isolated from the world's shadow-shader configuration.

## Anatomy: every appendage has a root

The grammar for every organic appendage is HOST BODY → EMBEDDED ROOT → ATTACHMENT MASS → TAPERED APPENDAGE (`CreatureAnatomy.ts`). It is shared by all four body plans; the plans only decide where on the torso things attach and how they are posed.

**Why limbs used to float.** Generator v1 placed each limb's top joint at a fixed lateral offset (`width·0.42 + stance·0.25`), which is outside the torso's half-width for most species, at the pelvis and chest joints — which were the two _ends_ of the torso sweep, where it tapered to a quarter of its width. The limb sweep then started exactly at that joint with a flat cap and dropped straight down, so legs read as separate cylinders beside the body. Hexapods attached their outer pairs to the same tapered ends.

**Limb roots.** Torsos now extend past the pelvis and chest (rump and chest-front path points), so limbs attach where the body is widest: fore limbs at the chest region (t = 0.75), hind limbs at the haunch (t = 0.25), hexapod pairs along the thorax, biped legs low on the pelvis bowl and arms at the upper chest. For each pair the +X limb is solved on the sampled host surface at `(t, angle)` — angle from the species' `shoulderSpread`/`hipSpread` — giving a surface point and outward normal; the −X limb is its exact mirror. The root is pushed inward along the normal by

`penetration = min(1.3·r · limbRootPenetration, ½ · distance(surface, host axis))`

where `r` is the limb radius and `limbRootPenetration` ≈ 0.62–0.85. The cap keeps the root from travelling more than half way to the torso axis; validation also requires normalised host depth between 0.3 and 0.97. The ball-joint pivot sits just inside the surface. The limb sweep runs root → pivot → knee → foot, so its first section travels outward-and-down along the surface normal before turning toward the ground, with 1.3× → 1.14× → 1× radius rings across the transition.

**Shoulder/hip masses.** Each limb gets one low-poly ellipsoid from inside the torso to ~42 % down the upper limb: long axis along that line, fore-aft radius `1.45 · r · mass · jointMassScale` (hips also scale with `haunchRoundness`), clamped to cover the limb tube and to stay within ~¾ of the local torso radius. Front masses are `shoulder`, rear `hip`; hexapods use small `limb-root` masses scaled by 0.4 so six roots never read as a row of balls. Masses are skinned from 80 % torso at their inner end to 12 % torso at the limb end.

**Skinning.** Chains use joint-centred weighting: each segment belongs to the bone at its start and mixes with its neighbour only near the joint (50/50 exactly at the joint), so limbs bend at knees rather than along their length. The limb's root segment blends torso → limb bone; together with the masses the shoulder deforms as a blend instead of a rigid cylinder pivoting beside the body. A test drives walk, run, turn and look poses and checks the embedded root ring follows the host bone exactly.

**Neck, head, tail.** Necks start inside the chest (thicker, 1.35× root) and end as a dome inside the head ellipsoid. Heads are their own short swept ellipsoid with domes both ends, an optional muzzle volume blended into the lower front, and a face frame (forward/up/right). Tails start inside the rump, leave just above it along the species' carriage and curl, taper from 1.18× to a domed tip, and never dip below the ground plane. Serpent bodies are thicker than legged torsos, curve in a gentle S, flatten underneath and carry an enlarged head on a short neck matching the body front.

**Limbs, feet and hands.** Upper limbs taper toward a knee/elbow with a subtle `jointScale` widening, lower limbs taper to the ankle (`limbTaper`). Bind poses spread shoulders/hips, bend fore limbs back and hind limbs forward, and return the foot under its support area; IK keeps each knee on the side recorded in the bind pose (`bendHint`), so knees never flip. Feet are hoof (flared, hard-edged sole), rounded paw, two-/three-toe or leaf, built on the y = 0 ground plane and kept level with the body during animation. Biped hands are mitten, paw, leaf or three-finger shapes.

**Features.** Ears, horns, antennae, frills, spikes, plates, fins and tail tips use one shape vocabulary — leaf, petal, rounded ear, spike, horn, fin, plate, diamond — with base, tip, width, thickness and a cheap 3-segment bend. Head features are placed from the face frame on the actual head surface; back rows ride the torso surface and blend with its spine bones; serpent fins are paired side fins solved on each side of the S-curved body. Every hosted feature's base is embedded below its surface point along the surface normal. Eyes are glossy dark ellipsoids sunk ~40 % into the head with a small emissive highlight.

**Colour and material.** One shared `MeshStandardMaterial` for every creature: vertex colours plus a per-vertex `surface` attribute (roughness, emissive) read by a shader hook composed through `shaderHooks.ts`, so CSM still owns its slot and glossy eyes, matte bodies (roughness 0.62–0.9) and eye highlights stay one draw call. Colour blocking comes from surface normals and semantic regions: primary body with a slightly deeper top, warm pale underside, pale muzzle, accent feet/hands and feature tips, horn tint, dark eyes and nose. Species patterns (gradient, stripe, spots, bands, limb-tips) are low-frequency and follow each part's own axis.

**Validation.** `validateCreatureAnatomy` (`CreatureAnatomyValidation.ts`) flags `FLOATING_LIMB`, `DEEP_LIMB_ROOT`, `FLOATING_MASS`, `FLOATING_NECK`, `FLOATING_HEAD`, `FLOATING_TAIL`, `FLOATING_FEATURE`, `BELOW_GROUND`, non-finite data, invalid bounds and unnormalised skin. Depth is measured against the sampled host volume (slabs between consecutive ring planes, normalised elliptical radius). It runs in unit tests (100 species per plan), in the Lab's GENERATE 100 report and in the Lab status line.

Animation directly evaluates semantic bones; there are no per-animal mixers or baked clips. Quadrupeds use phased walking, bipeds counter-swing their arms, hexapods alternate tripod groups, and serpents carry a travelling curvature wave. Idle breathing, head tracking, turn lean and tail sway share the rig. Lightweight two-bone leg IK samples existing world surfaces and preserves segment lengths. Unreachable terrain is clamped rather than stretching a limb. Root height follows terrain, and compiled ground offsets prevent size changes from burying the body.

## Terrain conformation: support plane, planted feet, per-leg IK

The body and the feet solve different problems (`CreatureGrounding.ts`). The **body** follows the overall terrain trend under its supporting feet; each **foot** keeps its own exact contact. Order per update: gait phase → step decisions and foot targets → surface samples → weighted best-fit support plane → desired body orientation and height → frame-rate-independent smoothing → gait bob/sway and head compensation on top → analytic two-bone IK to each contact.

**Support contacts.** Each leg (`GroundingLeg`, allocated once per runtime creature) is `planted` (support weight 1), `recovering` just after landing (0.4 → 1 over 0.12 s), `swing` (0 — a foot in the air never rotates the torso) or `seeking` (target rejected, e.g. deep water). A planted contact is fixed in world space until the foot lifts, so the body moves over it and feet do not skate. Feet lift when their gait phase (species phase offsets; duty factor 0.55–0.62) enters the swing window, or when the planted foot drifts from where the stance wants it by more than `stepTriggerDistance` (idle shuffles, turns). A foot that has drifted more than half its reach — after a sharp or fast turn — steps at once with a quicker swing, ignoring phase, step concurrency and its partner. New targets are the rest stance at the predicted landing pose plus half a stance of lead, sampled from the walkable surface; near creatures re-aim once mid-swing. Swings follow a smoothstep path plus a `sin(πt)` lift (`stepHeight`, slightly more when stepping up). Cadence rises when speed would demand a stride longer than 0.7 leg lengths.

**Weighted best-fit plane.** For N ≥ 3 support contacts (any leg count), a weighted least-squares fit of y = a·x + b·z + c, centred for stability; normal = normalize(−a, 1, −b). One robust pass: contacts further than 0.22 leg lengths from the plane are down-weighted by (threshold/residual)⁴ and the plane is refit once (a foot on a rock barely moves it). Two contacts fix the slope along their line; the normal comes from a 5-sample footprint plane (centre, front, rear, left, right — refreshed only when the body moves 0.25 leg lengths or every 0.5 s) projected perpendicular to that line. One contact uses the footprint plane; none keeps the previous orientation. Confidence (1 for a full support set, 0.5 two feet, 0.25 one foot) scales how fast the body responds, so gait transitions never switch algorithms abruptly.

**Orientation.** The support normal is decomposed into slope along the heading and across it, scaled by `bodyPitchFollowStrength` / `bodyRollFollowStrength` and clamped to `maxBodyPitch` / `maxBodyRoll`. Forward is the horizontal heading lifted vertically onto the resulting plane, so yaw is preserved exactly; right = up × forward. Heading is applied fresh every update and only the tilted up vector is smoothed (`1 − exp(−response·dt)`), which gives the same result at 30, 60 or 120 Hz.

**Height.** The root (ground point under the body) sits on the support plane, then one analytic correction: every supporting leg's hip must stay within [minReach, maxReach] of its foot target (vertical bounds solved in closed form), and pelvis/spine/chest must stay above the sampled ground by their torso's lower radius. Feet that are horizontally out of reach are left to step rather than chased; the body may dip at most 12 % of leg length for reach, and clearance always wins — this is what prevents the turn crouch. Height is smoothed with feed-forward along the previous plane, so climbing creatures do not trail below their legs.

**Feet, IK, heads.** IK targets put the sole (`soleOffset` = the bind foot height) on the contact; feet orient partly to the finite-difference surface normal (`footGroundAlignStrength`). Analytic two-bone IK clamps distance to the reachable range and keeps knees on their bind-pose side. Neck, head and — for bipeds — the upper torso counter-rotate part of the body pitch/roll (`neck/head/spineSlopeCompensation`).

**Body plans.** Quadrupeds and hexapods use the generic N-leg fit; tripod gaits keep three or more contacts most of the time and all six feet contribute over a cycle. Bipeds have two feet plus the footprint fallback, low roll follow (≈0.2) and moderate pitch. Serpents do not tilt as one body: 3–12 stations along the spine (by LOD) sample the ground, are low-pass filtered spatially ([1,2,1]) and temporally, and spine bones pitch so the body follows large contours with clearance while bridging small bumps.

**Species parameters** (`SpeciesLocomotion.terrain`, `CreatureTerrainProfile.ts`): `maxWalkSlope`, pitch/roll follow and limits, `bodyGroundResponse`, `footGroundAlignStrength`, `stepHeight`, `stepTriggerDistance`, `dutyFactor`, neck/head/spine compensation. Derived from plan, size and style (small animals climb ~8° steeper, giants ~8° less); optional in saves. Behaviour movement rejects ground steeper than `maxWalkSlope`; `canStandAtPose` (footprint fit, slope, standability, leg reach) and a support-polygon hull are exposed for future navigation.

**World and LOD.** Samples come from the authoritative `WorldSurfaceSampler` (terrain and foundation tops — a creature on a foundation levels out even on a hillside), never rendered meshes, through a per-update cache; feet never plant where deep water makes ground unstandable. Grounding follows the animation cadence: near creatures run full planting, IK, foot alignment and head compensation every frame; medium range (≈10 Hz) skips mid-swing retargeting and foot alignment; far creatures (≈2.5 Hz) only align the body to the footprint plane and use the stateless leg cycle. Grounding state survives LOD recompiles. No physics rigs, ragdolls or per-foot raycasts.

**Lab.** Developer controls add test grounds (flat, ramp 0–40°, uneven bumps with ramp, ramp with a flat foundation-like slab), walking uphill/downhill/across, a terrain-conformation on/off switch (off = the previous flat posing), live slope/pitch/roll/support telemetry, and overlays: foot targets coloured by state (green planted, yellow recovering, blue swing, red unreachable/invalid), support points, translucent support plane (red past the walkable limit) with normal and forward arrows, footprint fallback plane, IK chains, leg reach and body-clearance samples.

**Cost.** Per creature (Node, Apple M4): plane fit with outlier refit 0.15 µs; grounding update 1–4 µs; posing + IK 1.1–1.5× the previous flat posing (3–12 µs); 2.5–6 surface samples per update. In the browser stress scene (uneven 15° ramp), 100 grounded creatures need ≈0.2 ms/frame for support solving and ≈32,000 surface samples per second at 60 Hz, with total animation time within measurement noise of flat posing (`docs/creature-grounding-performance.json`).

## Ecology, behaviour and runtime

Species derive from world seed, 512-m ecology region and species slot. Population candidates derive from 64-m world cells, independent of rendered terrain chunks. Weighted size tiers keep giants rare. Social species spawn related individuals in loose groups. Regenerating a cell reproduces the same identities and recipes; leaving does not add ambient records to the save.

The explicit state machine supports IDLE, WANDER, LOOK, APPROACH, FLEE and RETURN_TO_GROUP. Decisions run at 2–5 Hz nearby and more slowly at distance; movement is separate from bone posing. Seek/arrive, bounded wandering, separation, cohesion and player awareness generate intent. Terrain and building probes reject unsafe targets and movement substeps; no combat or navmesh is added. `CreatureSpatialIndex` supplies neighbour queries. `CreatureEventBus` is an extension seam for future discovery/sound interactions.

`CreatureRuntimeManager` keeps a bounded population window. Only selected visible creatures receive live meshes. Quality budgets are centralized beside graphics presets: LOW 16, MEDIUM 25, HIGH 40 and ULTRA 50 maximum live creatures, further capped by the world setting (40 by default). Tiny animals disappear sooner; large animals can remain visible hundreds of metres away. Near rigs update each frame, medium rigs around 10 Hz, far rigs around 2.5 Hz. New compilation is limited to one creature per frame. New visuals grow in over 0.35 seconds after logical creation. Nearby shadow use follows the graphics preset. Coarse scaled body collision shapes keep player collision independent of skinned triangles.

World schema 5 introduced creature settings and persistent recipe overrides; later schema 6 adds furniture independently. Ambient creatures are regenerated. Lab-placed creatures are persistent and survive save/reload. The validator caps persistent overrides at 1,000, rejects runtime fields and conflicting/duplicate recipes, and preserves earlier music/building migrations.

## Creature Lab and verification

Open Creature Lab from the game or Creature settings, or standalone at `/creatures/` (`vite dev`, or `?dev` on a build; add `?inspect` to hide the Lab chrome). Change species/individual seeds, body plan, scale and LOD0/1/2; orbit or select front/side/top views; inspect wireframe, skeleton, collision, body chains, cross-sections, anchors and blended skin weights. GENERATE 100 compiles all three LODs for 100 species and reports validation (including anatomy issue counts), triangles per LOD and time. Place in world persists the recipe, including any Lab tuning. Creature settings also expose population density, active cap, debug controls and a deterministic five-species demo. Demo fauna is a development fixture, not the production distribution.

Developer-only controls (dev builds, `?dev`, or the standalone route — never normal players) add: a CURRENT/IMPROVED switch that lazily loads a frozen copy of the pre-rewrite generator (`creatures/legacy/`) for side-by-side comparison and benchmarking; overlays for limb roots (surface point, normal, embedded root, proximal bone), attachment volumes and joint/face frames, all riding their bones so they stay correct while animating; tuning multipliers for limb-root penetration, shoulder/hip mass, limb taper, joint scale, head, eye and muzzle scale; and a 12-species gallery for reviewing many seeds at once.

Unit coverage includes deterministic fingerprints, golden anatomy fingerprints for eight known seeds (`CreatureFingerprints.spec.ts`: key joints, attachment points, penetration, bounds, counts, geometry hash), 400 species and 4,000 bounded individuals, 100 validated species per plan, root penetration and attachment frames, mass dimensions, exact mirroring, torso-relative attachment across wide/narrow/deep/long/tiny/giant bodies, a negative `FLOATING_LIMB` case, skin normalisation and root blending, root rings riding their host bone through walk/run/turn/look, neck/tail/feature embedding, outward normals, finite geometry/normals/bounds at every LOD, legacy-recipe form derivation, LOD identity and triangle budgets, plus the earlier animation, ecology and persistence suites. Browser tests exercise all four Lab plans, 100-seed generation, placement/save/reload, streaming/return and graphics budgets; `tests/creatureQuality.e2e.ts` validates 100 species per plan in the browser, captures idle/walk/run screenshots with attachment overlays, and measures CURRENT vs IMPROVED cost.

## Limits and next milestone

Attachment masses overlap the torso rather than sharing a welded surface, so a soft intersection crease is visible up close (by design: no runtime CSG). Deep bends of very long legs still compress the blended region slightly. Hexapod and very thin biped legs can make the hip mass read as a small knob. Grounding does not climb stairs or ledges (the surface sampler allows it later), turns faster than a quick step still leave a foot briefly at full stretch, and there is no airborne/jump state yet. Coarse collision is intentionally conservative. Ambient individuals reset transient behaviour when their cells regenerate; persistent creatures retain recipe/position/state but not full simulation history. There is no indoor pathfinding, taming, breeding, combat, procedural fur or sound.

Next steps: stair/ledge stepping on authored surfaces, turn-anticipating foot placement, an impostor or culled tier beyond LOD2, optional per-vertex smoothing where masses meet the torso, and profiling real walking scenes on representative hardware.
